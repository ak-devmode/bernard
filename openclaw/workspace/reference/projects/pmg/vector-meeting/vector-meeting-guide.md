# Vector Meeting — Facilitation Guide

**Date:** Thursday, March 26, 2026 (afternoon)  
**Duration:** 2–2.5 hours  
**Participants:** Alex, Brant, Kezia, Okto, Gita, Pebri  
**Facilitator:** Alex  
**Goal:** Shared mental model of the opportunity space. Positive energy. Eyes open to connecting dots going forward. Structure Alex's forward-looking thinking with team input.

---

## 1. Opening — Set the Altitude (15 min)

Walk through the vision paper (key points, not a read-aloud). Establish the parallelogram concept. Make the vector metaphor concrete: "We're not picking an endpoint today. We're agreeing on a direction and a speed."

**Key message to land:** The things we're already building this year (PACS/ML, AI patient profiles, voice-to-profile) aren't just projects — they're building blocks for something much bigger. Today is about understanding what that bigger thing could be.

**Rule for the room:** For the first 30 minutes of discussion, no operational ideas. Nothing that starts with "we could use AI to..." followed by a task we already do manually. We're thinking about ##*what becomes possible*## — not about *what gets easier*. We'll come back to earth later.

---

## 2. Mapping the Parallelogram (60 min)

This is the core of the meeting. Work through each quadrant. Use a whiteboard or shared doc to capture data points as they come in.

### 2.1 Global Bleeding Edge — Now (15 min)

**Prompt:** "What is the most advanced healthcare technology actually deployed in clinical or commercial settings today — not in a lab, not in a paper, but working with real patients?"

Go around the room. Each person shares their 2–3 prepared examples. Capture on board.

**If the room goes quiet or stays small, Alex can seed with:**
- AI radiology tools (Viz.ai for stroke detection, Paige AI for pathology) already FDA-cleared and in routine clinical use in US hospitals
- Ambient clinical documentation (Nuance DAX, Abridge) — AI listens to doctor-patient conversations and writes the clinical note automatically. Live in thousands of US clinics.
- Continuous glucose monitors + AI coaching (Levels, Dexcom + AI layer) — real-time metabolic data feeding personalized health recommendations
- Surgical robotics (Intuitive's da Vinci, Medtronic Hugo) — robotic-assisted surgery now standard for many procedures, error rates declining year over year
- AI drug discovery (Insilico Medicine got a drug to Phase II clinical trials in record time, entirely AI-designed molecule)
- Remote patient monitoring at scale (Current Health / Best Buy Health, Biofourmis) — hospital-grade monitoring in the patient's home, AI-triggered escalation
- Digital pathology + AI (Proscia, PathAI) — slide scanning + ML analysis becoming standard workflow in advanced pathology labs

### 2.2 Indonesia Availability — Now (15 min)

**Prompt:** "Of these global capabilities — or others you've found — what's actually available and working in Indonesia today? What can you buy, deploy, or access here?"

**If the room goes quiet:**
- Halodoc / Alodokter — AI-assisted triage and teleconsultation (basic)
- BPJS digital integration — claims, referrals, basic digitization (clunky but functional)
- Siloam / Mayapada — some advanced imaging equipment but minimal AI layer
- SATU SEHAT pushing interoperability — regulatory intent for data sharing, early implementation
- WhatsApp-based patient engagement (everyone does this, nobody does it well)
- Basic EMR adoption growing but fragmented (many clinics still paper-based)
- Limited telemedicine reimbursement framework
- Some hospital groups piloting basic AI for imaging (very early stage)

### 2.3 Global Bleeding Edge — 5 Years (15 min)

**Prompt:** "Based on what you see now, where do you think the global edge will be in 2031? What's in development today that will be in production by then?"

**If the room goes quiet:**
- AI as primary diagnostician for routine imaging, pathology, and lab work — humans in oversight role (inversion of current model)
- Robotic surgery error rates at fractions of human equivalents; robotic systems handling routine procedures autonomously (with human standby)
- Longitudinal patient "digital twins" — AI models of individual patients that simulate treatment outcomes before they're tried
- Predictive health: AI flags disease risk years before symptoms based on behavioral, genomic, and continuous monitoring data
- Fully ambient clinical environments — no manual documentation, no data entry, AI handles all administrative workflow
- AI-powered insurance underwriting based on real-time health data rather than actuarial tables
- Decentralized diagnostics — lab-quality testing via handheld/portable devices + AI interpretation (already emerging with devices like Butterfly iQ for ultrasound)
- Synthetic data and federated learning enabling AI model training without sharing patient data across borders

### 2.4 Indonesia Availability — 5 Years (15 min)

**Prompt:** "Given regulatory pace, infrastructure, talent availability, and market readiness — what will actually be deployable in Indonesia by 2031?"

**If the room goes quiet:**
- SATU SEHAT interoperability likely mature — data sharing between providers becomes real
- AI-assisted imaging review probably available through international partnerships or local startups (this is what we're building now)
- Telemedicine regulatory framework likely settled — reimbursement pathways clearer
- EMR adoption widespread in urban clinics/hospitals (rural still lagging)
- WhatsApp / messaging-based care coordination significantly more sophisticated (AI-driven)
- Private supplemental insurance products more common (KMK 1366 regulatory direction)
- Some robotic surgical capability in top-tier Jakarta/Surabaya hospitals
- AI chatbot triage standard at most major hospital groups
- Portable/point-of-care diagnostics entering market (aligned with global trend but 2-3 years behind)
- Talent pool for health-tech development significantly larger (more CS/ML graduates, more exposure)

---

## 3. Where Is Padma? (20 min)

Now plot Padma on the parallelogram. Two exercises:

### 3.1 Where are we today?

**Prompt:** "Given what we just mapped — where does Padma sit right now? Inside the parallelogram? Outside it? Near which edge?"

Things to surface:
- Our PACS/ML work puts us ahead of almost all Indonesian providers on AI diagnostics
- Our patient profile / engagement work is ahead of the market
- Our clinical operations ground us in reality (we're not a pure tech play)
- Gaps: we're small, we're Bali-based, we don't have insurance licensing, our tech team is lean

### 3.2 What vector do we need?

**Prompt:** "What direction should we be moving? What capabilities should we be building or acquiring? How fast?"

This is the most open-ended part. Let the conversation flow. Capture themes, not decisions.

---

## 4. Thematic Streams (20 min)

Based on everything discussed, cluster the ideas into streams. These are not commitments — they're areas of strategic interest that we may choose to pursue, investigate further, or set aside.

**Candidate streams (pre-seeded, can be modified based on discussion):**

| Stream | Description | Time Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Diagnostics** | PACS/ML imaging, expand to pathology/lab. Own the diagnostic automation layer. | Already building (2026) |
| **Intelligent Patient Engagement** | AI profiles, voice-to-profile, personalized comms, automated care coordination. Deepen the relationship with each patient. | Already building (2026) |
| **Care Stratification** | AI triage → right-level response (AI / trained staff / doctor). Collapse cost of engagement while maintaining quality. | Near-term (2026–2027) |
| **Data & Risk Modeling** | Longitudinal patient data → predictive health insights → risk scoring. Foundation for managed care / insurance play. | Medium-term (2027–2028) |
| **Supplemental Coverage / Managed Care** | Use superior data + engagement + care delivery to offer BPJS-plus coverage model. The big play. | Long-term (2028–2031) |
| **Decentralized Diagnostics** | Point-of-care testing + AI interpretation. Extend diagnostic capability beyond clinic walls. | Explore (watch the market) |
| **Robotics & Advanced Procedures** | Monitor global progress. Not actionable near-term but relevant to 5-year view. | Watch (2028+) |

**For each stream, ask:**
1. Does this excite us? (Energy check)
2. Do we have or can we build the capability? (Feasibility check)
3. Does it move us in the right direction? (Vector check)

---

## 5. Close — Assignments and Energy (15 min)

**Recap:** What shared understanding did we build today? What surprised us?

**Assignments (if any emerge naturally):** People who want to dig deeper on a specific stream can volunteer to bring back more research for a follow-up.

**Final framing:** "The specific endpoint doesn't matter yet. What matters is that we're pointed in the right direction and moving at the right speed. Today was about calibrating the compass. We'll keep refining."

---

## Facilitator Notes for Alex

- **Watch for gravity.** The room will want to pull the conversation back to current operations. That's natural — redirect gently. "That's a great operational idea — let's capture it but stay at altitude for now."
- **Watch for hierarchy.** Gita and Pebri may defer to you and Brant. Explicitly invite them first in each round. They see things from the clinical and operational front line that you don't.
- **Watch for cynicism.** "That'll never work in Indonesia" is a valid data point but a bad opening move. If someone goes there, acknowledge the constraint and redirect: "You might be right about today — but what about 5 years? What would need to change?"
- **The vision paper does the heavy lifting.** If you share it before the meeting, people arrive with the altitude already set. The email primes the research. The meeting just needs to capture and connect.
- **Don't try to reach consensus.** This meeting succeeds if people leave with a shared mental model and energy, not if they agree on a plan.
- **Capture everything.** Assign someone (Kezia?) to take structured notes. You'll want them later.
