## 6. Regulatory Note: KMK 1366/2024 (Coordination of Benefits)

**On paper:** Private insurers can now cover cost differences when BPJS participants upgrade hospital class or access services beyond standard BPJS tariffs. This theoretically enables a "BPJS-plus" supplemental product.

**In practice (per PMG operational experience):** Episode-locking remains the real constraint. Once a patient begins a care episode in BPJS, leaving the system mid-episode invalidates coverage. Conversely, starting outside BPJS means you can't fall back into it. The regulation signals *intent* but the ground-level implementation hasn't caught up. No meaningful market activity on this yet.

**Implication for Padma:** The regulatory direction confirms the market wants a supplemental layer. The implementation gap means whoever figures out how to work *alongside* BPJS (not replace it) — helping patients navigate the system better + covering genuine gaps — has a first-mover window. This is exactly what a concierge/care coordination model does.
