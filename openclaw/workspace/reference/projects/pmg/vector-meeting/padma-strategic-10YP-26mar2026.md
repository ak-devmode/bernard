## 1. The Big Idea (10-Year Horizon)

- Healthcare and insurance are artificially separated. Technology (AI, continuous patient data, collapsed engagement costs) makes it possible to merge care delivery and coverage into one relationship.
- Indonesia: ~50M middle class + ~137M aspiring middle class, poorly served by both BPJS and private insurance.
- The bancassurance/unit-linked model dominating private coverage is structurally exploitative — high fees, opaque structures, optimized for bank distribution margins, not patient outcomes.
- The thesis: build care delivery, coordination, and data infrastructure bottom-up. At some point, add coverage layer (self-insure, acquire a licensing vehicle, or partner).
- The key insight from the vector meeting: AI compounds value *across* the stack when you own the full vertical (provider + orchestrator + payer). Separate market participants fight over profit pools and the patient loses. Vertical integration lets the compounding accrue to the system, not get sliced up.
- 10-year dream minimum. Complexity of entering insurance is not additive — it's multiplicative, possibly exponential. We are 100 miles from "bet the company." But the direction is set.

## 3. The Buried Lede: Crew Medical Data

- Expat concierge is interesting but small. Crew medical is where we have real volume.
- 20,000 medicals/year + $25M hospital spend = actual operating dataset with actuarial potential.
- What this data contains: conditions presenting, treatment costs by hospital, outcome variance, demographic patterns.
- Nobody else in Indonesia has this dataset at this granularity on this demographic.
- Any future coverage product's credibility gets built on this foundation, not the expat membership base.

## 4. The Grandfathered Pool

- One of PMG's silent owners holds a TPA contract for a marine benefits pool underwritten by Norwegian Hull Club.
- Grandfathered licensing from historical Indo-Norwegian political connection (seafaring families).
- Fits our demographic. Currently run poorly.
- Unknowns: number of insureds, loss ratios, licensing structure, OJK conditions, transferability.
- Strategic significance: if real and accessible, this shortcuts the TPA path by years — no need to negotiate from scratch with an established insurer or pursue an OJK license independently.
- Worth investigating carefully, not rushing.

- Padma Care continues as B2C R&D lab

- Investigate grandfathered marine pool (licensing, financials, operational state, acquisition path)
- Begin care stratification: AI triage → right-level response (AI / trained staff / doctor) to collapse engagement costs

**Long-term (2028–2031):**
- From TPA vantage, evaluate which part of insurance value chain is interesting / accessible / profitable / ripe for disruption
- Build or acquire data + risk modeling capability to credibly price supplemental coverage
- Develop BPJS-plus model leveraging KMK 1366 (coordination of benefits) — only when episode-locking implementation gap closes enough to be practical
- Decide: self-insure, acquire licensed vehicle, or remain TPA/MGA with partner holding regulatory wrapper

**The MGA/TPA path is the most capital-efficient route to the top layer.** Own the member relationship, care delivery, data, product design. Let a licensed insurer provide balance sheet + regulatory wrapper. Evaluate from operational knowledge, not financial speculation.

## 6. Key Strategic Constraints

**The 100% rule.** Indonesian consumer businesses require replacement to be 100% better than incumbent, not 25-30%. US-style early adopters who try anything for marginal improvement don't exist here. No half-baked BPJS-plus product launched to iterate. Must be overwhelmingly better on day one.

**The expat-to-Indonesian pivot is a second company.** Different customers, trust frameworks, channels, pricing psychology, willingness to pay for service vs. product. Bottom-up capabilities transfer (coordination, network, AI tooling). Go-to-market is a fresh start. Crew medical business is a more natural bridge to the Indonesian market than expat concierge.

**The data moat needs scale.** A few hundred expat patients ≠ actuarial credibility. The crew medical dataset (20K medicals/year, $25M hospital spend) is where scale already exists.

**Insurance is a business we know nothing about.** Three structural barriers surfaced in the vector meeting:
- Pool size: 40 concierge members is not an insurance pool. Need to buy into or build a much larger base.
- OJK regulation: heavily regulated, allowable rates, extensive compliance, slow-moving innovation environment.
- Float management: Indonesian regulations require float to remain onshore where returns are inferior and higher-risk vs. international. Constrains the economics of any insurance play.
- Additionally: claims adjudication, fraud detection, provider network management at scale, reserve management, reinsurance — all net-new capabilities.

**The BPOM/pharma gap is widening, not closing.** Approval process is static while innovation accelerates and TAMs narrow (making approval investment uneconomic for niche therapies). Padma's care advisory role ("you need to go to Singapore for this") becomes *more* valuable over time. Structural tailwind for the concierge model.
