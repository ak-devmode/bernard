## 1. Document Purpose & Context

### 1.1 What This Document Is

A single-page emergency reference card delivered to new Padma Care members during Week 2 of onboarding. The core value: a three-tier triage guide that tells members *which facility to go to* based on severity — information that is genuinely localized, life-relevant, and not available via Google.

### 1.2 Why This Document Matters — The Honest Context

Padma Care cannot help during an active ER situation. ERs in Bali are hostile to outside influence, particularly in acute cases. Padma can't get you there faster, can't triage you, and forcing access to the ER risks broader hospital relationships that matter for inpatient advocacy later.

What Padma *can* do: ensure you have a plan *before* something happens so you go to the right place, and then bring the full advocacy service to bear once you're stabilized and admitted. The Day 0 plan bridges the gap between emergency and inpatient care — where Padma's value begins.

The other critical context: many expats believe they can "just fly out" if something serious happens. In reality, medical evacuation is only viable in a narrow set of cases and typically happens on day 2-3 after stabilization, not day 0. Having a local emergency plan isn't optional — it's the plan for the first 48-72 hours regardless of what happens next.

### 1.3 When It Gets Used

Two moments:
1. **Delivery moment (Week 2):** PHA sends via WhatsApp. Member saves to phone, prints, puts on fridge. The PHA message carries the warmth — the document itself stays utilitarian.
2. **Use moment (emergency):** The member (or their partner, housekeeper, driver, nanny) pulls this out under stress and makes a decision about where to go.

### 1.4 What It Must Do

1. **Tell them where to go based on severity** — three tiers, clear decision logic
2. **Give them Padma Care contact info** — message us on the way so we're ready when you're admitted
3. **Provide an actionable checklist** — what to bring, what to do, what not to do
4. **Counteract the "I'll just fly out" misconception** — briefly, without scaremongering

### 1.5 What It Must NOT Do

- Suggest Padma can help in the ER itself — that's not honest and not operationally true
- Be cluttered — in a panic, every extra element is noise
- Include marketing language — this is a utility document, not a sales tool
- Use small type — must be readable without glasses, in poor lighting, shaking hands

## 2. Design Direction

### 2.1 Format

- Single A4 page, single-sided (front only)
- PDF optimized for WhatsApp sending (under 2MB, vector graphics + text only)
- Must be legible: on phone screen, printed on home inkjet, photocopied in B&W
- Same Crew Care design family but utilitarian — less decorative, more functional

### 2.2 Color & Contrast

- High contrast priority — dark text on light background for all critical info
- #505CA0 (blue-purple) for headers/structure
- #E6854B (burnt orange) for phone numbers and the "severe" tier
- Green for "minor" tier, amber/orange for "moderate" tier, red for "severe" tier — traffic-light logic
- Year tag: full-bleed accent stripe on the side with "2026" — allows annual refresh and gives Padma a reason to re-engage ("here's your updated plan for 2027")

### 2.3 Visual Approach

Think: airplane safety card meets medical triage poster. Clean, scannable, three clear zones. No photographs. Icons for each tier (bandage, hospital, heart/brain). The three-tier block is the hero of the page — it should occupy the largest visual space.

Design note: The three tiers should be immediately scannable — a person in panic should be able to look at this and identify which tier they're in within 3 seconds. Color coding does the heavy lifting. Tier 3 should be visually the most urgent (red/bold) but all three tiers need equal visual clarity.

The key insight on Tier 3: for severe cardio/neuro cases, going to a closer ER first often means a junior doctor assesses, calls a senior, and then transfers — burning time. Going directly to Prof Ngurah gives you the senior team and OR access immediately.

### 3.3 Block 2: What to Do — Checklist

Compact. Sits below or beside the triage guide.

> **WHEN HEADING TO THE ER:**
>
> 1. **Go by car.** Taxi, your driver, a neighbor — don't wait for an ambulance. (Ambulance: 118 / 119 — response is unreliable.)
>
> 2. **Message Padma Care on the way:** +6282266323030 — "emergency, heading to [hospital]." Don't wait for a reply. We'll be ready when you need us.
>
> 3. **Bring:** passport, KITAS/KITAP, insurance card (or policy number + insurer emergency line). No insurance? Don't worry — we handle pricing.
>
> 4. **Bring your medication list.** Your PHA has a copy — ask them to send it if you don't have it handy.
>
> 5. **Don't sign anything you don't understand.** Admission forms are in Indonesian. It's OK to say "my medical team is on the way."
>
> 6. **Ask for kelas 1 or VIP** if admitted. You can always downgrade. Upgrading later is hard.

Design note: Six items is the max. Each should be one line of bold instruction + one line of explanation. The "go by car" guidance replaces a false sense of security from listing unreliable ambulance numbers.

### 3.4 Block 3: Evacuation Note

Small, factual block. Not alarmist. Counters a common misconception.

Design note: This is 2-3 sentences max. It's not a selling point — it's an honest framing that justifies the existence of this card. Many expats believe "I'll just fly out" and therefore don't need a local plan. This gently corrects that without scaremongering.

## 4. Personalization

### 4.1 What's Actually Personalized

Honest assessment: personalization is aspirational at this stage. The three-tier triage guide is the same for most of Bali — Prof Ngurah is always the Tier 3 destination, and the Tier 2 primary ER varies by area but there are only 5-6 options across the island.

Realistic personalization per member:

| Field | Personalized? | Notes |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| Member name | Yes | In the header |
| PHA name + number | Yes | Contact block |
| Tier 2 primary ER | Yes, by area | ~5-6 area variants covers everyone |
| Tier 1 local clinic | Aspirational | Could be generic "your nearest 24hr clinic" for now |
| Year | Yes (2026) | Updated annually |

### 4.2 Production Process

The area-based Tier 2 ER is the only content that changes between members in the same area. This means you need ~5-6 template variants (one per area), not one per member.

Recommended approach: create 5-6 area-specific Canva templates. PHA duplicates the right one for the member's area, adds member name and PHA info, exports as PDF, sends via WhatsApp. Under 5 minutes per member.

## 5. Design Constraints

### 5.1 Phone Readability

Many members will view this PDF on their phone screen during an emergency. The three tiers must be distinguishable at phone zoom. Phone numbers must be tappable.

### 5.2 Print Survivability

Members may print on a home inkjet. Must look good on basic printers. Must survive being folded and stuffed in a drawer for months.

### 5.3 B&W Reproduction

The three-tier color coding is the primary visual hierarchy. It MUST also work in grayscale — test by printing in B&W. If the tiers aren't distinguishable without color, add secondary visual cues (tier labels in different sizes, border weights, or fill patterns).

### 5.4 File Size

Under 2MB for WhatsApp. Vector graphics and text only. No embedded photos.

### 6.2 Ambulance Reality

118 (national) and 119 (emergency) are included on the document but with a caveat. The previously reliable private dispatch (0361 251 177) appears to have shut down in 2026 — tested 3x this year with only 1 response. The honest guidance is "go by car" and this is what the document says. If a reliable private ambulance provider emerges, add that number.

### 6.3 Burn Cases

There is no dedicated burn unit outside Prof Ngurah. Severe burns are ultimately a Singapore evacuation case if the patient has coverage/funds — but stabilization happens locally first. This is too detailed for the card. If a member has specific burn risk concerns (restaurant owner, etc.), the PHA can address verbally.

### 6.4 Pediatric Emergencies

Bunda (not Puri Bunda) has pediatric ER capabilities. However, for most acute pediatric cases, getting to a full ER faster is better than driving further for a pediatric-specific one. Not included on the card — too narrow for a general-purpose document. PHA can advise case-by-case.

### 6.5 ER Culture Context (Internal — Informs Why the Card Exists)

Not for the card, but important for anyone creating or explaining this document:

ERs in Bali operate with rigid SOP adherence and strong deference to the senior attending physician. Junior doctors translate the situation up rather than acting autonomously, even when time matters. There is no meaningful malpractice liability driving this behavior — it's cultural and institutional. In acute situations, doctors may get consumed by visible injuries (wounds, blood, exposed bone) rather than following ABCD triage protocol (airway, breathing, circulation, disability). This is why *which ER* matters — the primary ERs (Tier 2) have more experienced staff who can exercise some autonomous judgment, and Prof Ngurah (Tier 3) has the specialists on standby to skip the assess-then-transfer delay entirely.

ERs are also unreceptive to outside influence. Padma cannot intervene in the ER, and attempting to do so risks broader hospital access. This is why the card says "message us on the way" rather than "call us first" — Padma's value begins at stabilization and admission, not in the ER itself.

### 6.6 Medication List Dependency

The checklist tells members to bring their medication list and that the PHA has a copy. This requires the PHA to actually have one on file. For members onboarded before the Day 30 voice-note medical history capture, the PHA should proactively request a medication list when delivering the Day 0 plan.

### 6.7 Annual Refresh

The year tag (2026) creates a built-in annual touchpoint. Each year, Padma sends an updated card — new year tag, any ER changes, updated PHA info if applicable. This is a natural, non-transactional re-engagement moment that reinforces value. Add to the onboarding playbook (marketing plan Priority 2) as a recurring annual task.

### 6.8 After-Hours Honesty

Padma Care operates 8am–8pm. The card is transparent about this. The guidance is: "head to your ER and message us — we'll follow up first thing." If Padma ever introduces an after-hours duty line, update this card — that would be a significant membership value-add and should be highlighted prominently.
