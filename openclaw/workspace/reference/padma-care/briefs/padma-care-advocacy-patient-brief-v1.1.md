## 1. Document Purpose & Context

### 1.1 What This Document Is

A single-page (front only) PDF designed to convert a frustrated hospital patient or their family member into an advocacy client. This is a selling document — but one that sells through empathy and clarity, not pressure.

### 1.2 When It Gets Used

A doctor, surgeon, or hospital staff member hands this to a patient or family member who is visibly struggling with their hospital experience — confused about treatment, frustrated with communication, overwhelmed by the system. The referral happens at the point of need, not proactively.

This means the reader is:
- Already in hospital (admitted or family of someone admitted)
- Emotionally activated — some combination of confusion, frustration, fear, anger, or helplessness
- Not browsing or comparison shopping — they need help now
- Possibly holding this piece of paper while sitting in a hospital corridor

### 1.3 What It Must Do (in order of priority)

1. **Validate their experience** — "what you're feeling is normal, and it's not your fault"
2. **Name the structural gap** — the system wasn't built for them, without blaming the hospital or doctor
3. **Show what Padma does** — concrete, immediate, tangible
4. **Make contact effortless** — one WhatsApp message and help arrives
5. **Remove price anxiety** — show what it costs upfront, no surprises

### 1.4 What It Must NOT Do

- Criticize the hospital, doctor, or Indonesian healthcare system
- Feel like ambulance chasing or predatory sales
- Overwhelm with options — this person is already overwhelmed
- Use clinical jargon (inpatient, triage, care coordination)
- Promise outcomes ("we will fix everything") — promise clarity and support

## 2. Design Direction

### 2.1 Format

- Single A4 page, single-sided (front only)
- Print-ready + digital (WhatsApp-sendable PDF)
- Same Crew Care design family as the concierge brochure
- Color palette: #505CA0 (blue-purple primary), #FFD4C4 (light salmon secondary), #E6854B (burnt orange CTA accent)

### 2.2 Tone Difference from Concierge Brochure

The concierge brochure is confident and upbeat — "your insider healthcare network." This document is calmer, steadier, more direct. Think: the voice of a competent friend who just showed up and said "OK, let me help you figure this out." Not cheerful. Not somber. Just clear.

### 2.3 Visual Notes

- Less photography, more white space — the reader is stressed, don't add visual noise
- If a photo is used, it should be a Padma nurse or case manager in a hospital setting — someone who looks like they belong there and know what they're doing
- The WhatsApp CTA should be the single most prominent element on the page after the headline
- Consider QR code to WhatsApp contact for easy phone scanning

## 3. Content — Section by Section

### 3.1 Header

### 3.2 Headline

> You shouldn't have to figure out a healthcare system while you're inside it.

Design note: This is the lead. Large type. It does all the emotional work — it names the absurdity of the situation without blaming anyone. The reader should feel seen immediately. Alternative headline for testing: "The healthcare system here wasn't built for us. Padma is."

### 3.3 Opening Paragraph

> Being in hospital is hard enough. Understanding what's happening with your treatment, knowing your options, making sure bills are fair, and communicating with doctors across a language barrier — that shouldn't be your job right now. Padma Care's advocacy team handles all of it, starting today.

Design note: Short paragraph, direct. No "we understand how you feel" — that's empty. Instead, name the specific things that are hard and then say "we handle that."

Five short, scannable items. Icon + 1 sentence each. Vertical list (not a grid — easier to scan when stressed).

**2. Your Options, Clearly Explained**
> Second opinions, alternative treatments, transfer options — we lay out what's available so you can decide with confidence.

**3. Bill Review & Pricing Advocacy**
> We vet every charge, challenge anything that doesn't look right, and make sure you're paying fair rates.

**4. Communication Bridge**
> We speak the language, know the system, and make sure nothing gets lost between you and your medical team.

**5. Documentation You Can Use**
> Every case gets a comprehensive English medical report — usable for insurance claims, follow-up doctors, or your own records.

A small but important block. This addresses the "I'm not ready to commit to paying for something" hesitation.

Design note: This block should feel generous, not salesy. Light background, smaller type than the main content. It's the "foot in the door" that demonstrates value before asking for money.

> **Advocacy Onboarding** (getting our team up to speed on your case): **IDR 1,000,000** one-time

Design note: Don't list the entire advocacy menu from the brochure here — that's too much for someone in a hospital bed. Show the core price (onboarding + daily rate) and signal that more is available. Keep it clean.

### 3.6.1 Evacuation Note (small, factual — 1-2 sentences)

Design note: This addresses the most common question from panicking inpatients ("can I just fly out of here?") without being alarmist. It positions Padma as the people who can actually answer that question, which is a strong reason to engage. One or two sentences max.

Design note: This testimonial works because it implies ongoing, real-world use across multiple medical situations — exactly what a scared hospital patient needs to hear. Keep it small. If space is tight, cut it — the WhatsApp CTA matters more than the testimonial on this document.

## 4. Messaging Principles (Advocacy-Specific)

These supplement the general principles in the concierge brochure brief (Section 5).

### 4.1 Core Insight

From the marketing plan (Section 3.3.2.4): advocacy patients are not experiencing peace. They are experiencing loss of control, confusion, and often fear or anger. The value Padma brings is not comfort — it's clarity. Clarity restores agency. We don't manage their emotions; we give them the information to make decisions.

### 4.2 The Framing That Does All the Work

"The healthcare system here wasn't built for us." This single line:
- Names the structural gap (not the hospital's fault, not the patient's fault)
- Puts Padma and the patient on the same team ("us" not "you")
- Is non-adversarial toward hospitals and doctors
- Lets every patient project their own specific frustration onto it

### 4.3 Do

- Name specific, concrete frustrations (bills, communication, options, documentation)
- Make contact feel like one step away — WhatsApp message and help arrives
- Acknowledge the family member doing the worrying from outside the room
- Position the free orientation as genuine value, not a sales hook

### 4.4 Don't

- Say "we understand how you feel" — you don't, and it sounds hollow
- Promise outcomes — promise process (clarity, information, advocacy)
- Use the word "crisis" or "emergency" — too dramatic for the tone
- List too many services — the full menu lives on the brochure, not here
- Criticize any specific hospital, doctor, or the Indonesian system broadly

## 5. Distribution Notes

### 5.1 Doctor Handoff

The primary distribution channel is doctors handing this to patients. This means:
- The document should look professional enough that a doctor isn't embarrassed to hand it over
- It should NOT look like a discount flyer or promotional material
- It should feel like a resource the doctor is sharing because they genuinely think it will help

### 5.2 Hospital Staff

Secondary channel: hospital staff (nurses, front desk, patient liaisons) who encounter frustrated foreign patients. Same quality bar applies.

### 5.3 Digital Distribution

The PDF should be optimized for WhatsApp forwarding — reasonable file size, readable on phone screen. Consider that some recipients will view this on a phone in a hospital bed, possibly without glasses.

### 5.4 Print Considerations

Should be printable on a standard office printer without losing legibility. Consider that hospitals may photocopy it — make sure it survives black-and-white reproduction (test: does the hierarchy still work without color?).
