## 1. Context and Constraints

Padma Care operates two service tracks with distinct customer profiles:

**Healthcare Concierge** — out-of-hospital care coordination, peace of mind for everyday health needs. Recurring membership model. Target: long-term expat residents, working age and retiree, who want a trusted healthcare partner before something goes wrong.

**Healthcare Advocacy** — in-hospital navigation, BPJS complexity, dealing with Indonesian medical bureaucracy when something has already gone wrong. Target: long-term residents and acutely ill/injured expats who are in crisis or recently discharged.

These two tracks share a target market (long-term residents) but have different urgency profiles and different discovery moments. Channel strategy must account for both.

**What has been tested and ruled out:**

Villa and short-term rental concierge networks — low LTV, wrong product fit, food poisoning one-day ER visits are not the target patient. Removed from consideration.

Hospital front-office staff recruitment — too high a risk of poisoning hospital relationships that are operationally necessary for the advocacy product.

Hospital management partnerships — current experience is that management believes they already have programs. Not worth continued investment until there's a specific relationship change.

**Core tension:** The highest-intent prospects (people in a hospital waiting room right now) are in a moment of stress and vulnerability. Unsolicited outreach in that moment feels predatory regardless of intent. The better investment is being found *before* they end up there.

## 2. Target Segments

Understanding which segment a channel reaches determines priority and message.

**Segment A — Long-term resident, working age (30–55), digitally active.** Digital nomads, remote workers, entrepreneurs, younger families. Active on Instagram, Facebook groups, Reddit, WhatsApp communities. Respond to peer recommendations and demonstrated expertise. Higher Concierge conversion potential. Discovery happens online and through community.

**Segment B — Long-term resident, retiree (55+), lower digital engagement.** Retired expats, often on longer-term visas, frequently with existing health conditions. Less social media, more Facebook than Instagram, active in physical community organizations (Rotary, expat societies, church groups). Respond to credibility established in person. Higher Advocacy conversion potential given health profile. Discovery happens through community organizations and GP relationships.

**Segment C — Acute need, any demographic.** Someone currently in crisis — hospitalized, injured, recently discharged, facing a bureaucratic wall. Hardest to reach proactively by definition. Reachable only through search (GEO/SEO) and geofenced paid media near hospitals. Highest urgency, highest immediate value, lowest volume.

**Segment D — Healthcare-adjacent professionals.** Nurses, front-office staff, GPs at international clinics, hospital social workers. Not patients — potential referral relationships. Reached through professional channels and geofenced B2B messaging.

## 4. Channel Detail

### 4.1 Online Community Participation
**Segments:** A primarily, some B on Facebook  
**Complexity:** Low — Bernard-assisted (Section 10 of master plan)  
**Impact:** High, compounds over time

The community intelligence engine documented in the Bernard master plan. Bernard monitors Facebook groups, Reddit, WhatsApp (via team member), TripAdvisor forums. Surfaces threads worth joining. Drafts responses in Padma Care voice. Human reviews and posts.

The voice principle that works: lead with domain knowledge, answer completely, be clear about the free/paid line without apology. Do not pitch in emergency threads. Consistent presence over months builds reputation that drives inbound.

Platform priority order:
1. Facebook expat groups (Bali Expats, Seminyak/Canggu/Ubud community groups) — Segment A and B both present
2. Reddit r/bali, r/expats — Segment A, higher intent questions
3. WhatsApp groups — requires human team member presence, agent gets summaries
4. TripAdvisor Bali health/medical forums — lower volume, high intent

**What success looks like:** 3–5 quality community responses per week, consistent presence over 3+ months, inbound DMs from community members. Not viral reach — relationship equity.

**Bernard dependency:** Phase 3 in master plan. Vault must contain voice guide and free/paid line doc before activating.

### 4.2 GEO/AI Search Content
**Segments:** A, C  
**Complexity:** Low-Medium — content production is the work  
**Impact:** High, compounds over time, increasingly important as AI search grows

The goal is not Google PageRank. The goal is being the cited answer when someone asks an AI assistant or does a specific long-tail search about a problem they're experiencing in Bali. AI assistants (Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity) reward depth, specificity, and direct answers to specific questions. A well-written page that answers "what do I do if I'm hospitalized in Bali and I don't speak Indonesian" can surface in AI responses within weeks.

Content strategy: intercept at the problem level, not the solution level. Someone searching "how to find a good doctor in Bali" is not searching for Padma Care — they don't know it exists. Someone searching "BPJS as a foreigner what does it actually cover" is exactly the person who needs the advocacy product.

Content priority queue (Bernard surfaces from community monitoring):
- Indonesian hospital admission process for foreigners — step by step
- What BPJS covers and doesn't cover for expats
- How to get a second opinion in Indonesia
- What to do in a medical emergency in Bali (not food poisoning — actual emergency)
- Understanding hospital billing in Indonesia
- How to get medical records translated and certified
- Evacuation insurance — what it does and doesn't do
- Finding an English-speaking specialist in Bali

Format: long-form FAQ structure. Direct answers to specific questions. Dr. Gita or Alex attributed for credibility. Updated periodically. No fluff.

**What success looks like:** Padma Care pages appearing in AI search responses for Bali expat health queries within 3–6 months. Inbound contacts referencing finding the site via search.

**Bernard dependency:** Content queue tracking in vault. Bernard surfaces briefs, human writes or reviews drafts.

### 4.3 Dr. Gita Community Speaking
**Segments:** B primarily  
**Complexity:** Low — relationship outreach to existing organizations  
**Impact:** High for retiree segment, no substitute for this channel

Retirees respond to credibility demonstrated in person by someone they've met. This is the only channel that reliably reaches Segment B at the trust level required for a healthcare membership product. No digital channel replicates it.

The format that works: Dr. Gita speaks for 40–45 minutes on a genuinely useful topic. No pitch. Business cards on chairs. Time for questions. The talk itself is the value demonstration.

Topic examples that work for this audience:
- Understanding your health coverage in Indonesia — what your policy actually pays for
- Navigating Indonesian hospitals as a foreigner — what to expect, what to ask
- Managing chronic conditions in Bali — finding the right specialists
- What to do when a family member is hospitalized and you're the decision-maker

Target organizations (Bali):
- Bali Expat Society
- Rotary Club Bali (multiple chapters)
- International Women's Association Bali
- Seminyak, Sanur, Ubud expat community groups
- International church congregations (significant retiree expat attendance)
- Specific residential communities with high long-term expat density (Sanur, Seminyak)

**Outreach approach:** Direct email to organization committee, offering a free speaker on healthcare navigation for expats. These organizations actively seek speakers with genuine expertise. Not a sales conversation — a community service offer.

**What success looks like:** 1–2 speaking engagements per month. Post-talk inbound inquiries. Membership conversions traceable to events within 30–60 days.

**Execution owner:** Dr. Gita + Kezia for outreach and scheduling. Alex provides content support.

### 4.4 Geofenced Meta Ads — Patient Targeting
**Segments:** A, C  
**Complexity:** Medium — requires campaign setup, creative, ongoing management  
**Impact:** Medium-High, especially for Segment C acute need

Drop a 1-mile radius pin on BIMC Kuta, Kasih Ibu, Siloam Denpasar, and any other hospitals with significant expat traffic. Target people physically near those locations who match long-term resident profile: age 35+, English-language, expat behavioral segment, interests correlating with residency not tourism.

Meta's expat behavioral targeting is reasonably accurate. Facebook over Instagram for the older end of the audience. The acute-need targeting won't catch people in real-time (Meta is not real-time geofencing) but dwell time at hospitals is long enough and the targeting window broad enough to be functionally useful.

**Message for this audience:** Not a membership pitch. A useful resource offer. "Navigating an Indonesian hospital as a foreigner? We've helped hundreds of expats through this." CTA to a free guide download or a no-cost 15-minute triage call. Capture email/WhatsApp. Follow up with human.

**Creative direction:** Dr. Gita on camera. Warm, direct, knowledgeable. Not polished production — authenticity matters more for this audience than a TV-quality ad. Short (15–30 seconds) for Instagram. Longer form possible for Facebook feed.

**Budget:** Start small — test with $300–500/month across 3–4 hospital locations. Measure cost per lead, not cost per click. Optimize toward inquiry conversion.

**Bernard dependency:** Ad copy drafting, performance monitoring, surfacing what's working. Campaign management still requires human judgment on budget and creative decisions.

### 4.5 Geofenced Meta Ads — Staff/Professional Targeting
**Segments:** D  
**Complexity:** Medium  
**Impact:** Medium — referral channel, not direct conversion

Same hospital geofence locations, different audience: Indonesian language, healthcare worker interests, possibly job title targeting. Message is entirely different — this is a professional tool, not a patient pitch. "When your foreign patients need more support than you can provide, we handle the coordination." Positions Padma Care as a resource that makes their job easier, not a competitor.

LinkedIn is more precise for job title targeting but significantly more expensive. Meta with interest-based healthcare worker targeting is a reasonable starting point. Test before committing budget.

**What success looks like:** Inbound from nurses or clinic staff asking about patient referrals. Even one or two quality referral relationships from this channel justifies the spend.

### 4.6 GP and Specialist Referral Relationships
**Segments:** B primarily  
**Complexity:** Medium — relationship-based, requires Dr. Gita's time  
**Impact:** High for retiree segment

Long-term expat retirees have existing GP relationships at international clinics. If their GP knows what Padma Care does and can mention it when relevant — "if you ever need help navigating the hospital system, there's a service called Padma Care" — that's a warm introduction into exactly the right cohort.

This is not a formal referral arrangement. It's a professional relationship where GPs understand the service and feel comfortable mentioning it. The conversation is a 30-minute visit to English-speaking GPs at BIMC, Kasih Ibu Bali, Siloam, and the independent international clinics. Bring a one-pager that explains what Padma Care does clearly — not a brochure, a professional document that a GP can file.

**Target clinics for initial outreach:** International clinics where English-speaking GPs see long-term expat patients regularly. Not ER docs — GPs with ongoing patient relationships.

**What success looks like:** 3–5 GPs who understand the service and mention it when relevant. Not a formal referral program. Informal professional relationships.

**Execution owner:** Dr. Gita. This is peer-to-peer medical professional outreach.

### 4.9 Physical Guide at Clinics and Pharmacies
**Segments:** C, D  
**Complexity:** Low-Medium — design and distribution  
**Impact:** Medium — long tail, hard to measure

A one-page "foreigner's guide to navigating Indonesian hospital admission" in English and Bahasa. Practical, specific, actionable. Small Padma Care brand mention on the back with QR code to the free triage call offer. Not a brochure — a resource.

The pitch to clinic staff is "we made something useful for your foreign patients." Harder to refuse than a referral arrangement, lower risk than recruiting staff. Placement: waiting areas at international pharmacies (Guardian, Century), independent GP clinics, any clinic where staff have expressed sympathy for the problem.

The QR code matters more than the paper. It captures an intent signal at exactly the right moment.

### 4.13 Meta Paid Campaign — Audit, Optimization and Agentic Management

**Segments:** A, C  
**Complexity:** Medium — API integration into pmg-integrations bus  
**Impact:** Medium-High once funnel is correctly configured

#### 4.13.1 Existing Campaign Audit (First Order Task)

~$400 was spent Dec–Feb producing ~500k impressions and approximately 1 lead. Before any new spend, the existing data must be diagnosed. The CSV export from Meta Ads Manager will be analyzed to identify where the funnel leaked.

**Audit framework — pull these numbers for each ad set and creative:**

Impressions → Clicks (CTR). Below 0.5% means the creative or targeting wasn't resonating with the audience shown. Above 1% means the ad worked and the problem is downstream.

Clicks → Landing page views. A significant drop here indicates load time issues or a message mismatch between ad promise and landing page delivery.

Landing page views → CTA action. Track drop-off at each stage. The stage 3 Calendly CTA was correctly placed for a warm retargeted audience — the issue was the quality of the audience that made it to stage 3, not the CTA itself.

**Corrected understanding of the Dec–Feb campaign:**

The campaign was actually a correctly-architected three-stage retargeting sequence: Stage 1 drove cold audience to the website. Stage 2 retargeted website visitors. Stage 3 retargeted stage 2 engagers with the Calendly CTA. This is the right structural approach for this product — the funnel architecture was sound and the Calendly placement was appropriate for a third-touch warm audience.

The problem was upstream contamination, not structural. CPM of ~$0.80 on 500k impressions indicates a broad, non-geofenced, low-competition audience — likely pulling Indonesian locals and tourists into the stage 1 website visitor pool. Meta retargeting builds every downstream audience from whoever engaged with stage 1. If those people were the wrong demographic, every retargeting pool was polluted. By the time stage 3 ran, the Calendly was being shown to a pool that may have been a few hundred people who were never likely to convert regardless of the CTA.

The three-stage sequence logic is preserved for the next campaign. It just needs a qualified audience at the top. Geofencing with behavioral layering fixes the upstream contamination — stage 1 starts with people who were physically near a hospital and match the long-term expat profile, so every retargeting pool downstream is built on real signal.

**Key audit questions for the CSV:** How large was the retargeting pool at each stage (reach, not impressions)? Was stage 2 reach above 2,000 unique people? What was the CTR at each stage? Was there a large drop in reach between stages indicating the pools were too small?

**Action:** Drop the Dec–Feb CSV into a Claude context window for detailed analysis. Output: funnel leak diagnosis by stage, reach and pool size at each retargeting level, CPM/CTR/CPC breakdown by ad set and creative, confirmation or revision of the upstream contamination hypothesis, recommended targeting parameters for next test.

#### 4.13.2 Corrected Funnel Model

The correct conversion funnel for Padma Care paid acquisition is not ad → Calendly. It is:

Ad (problem-aware message) → Low-friction value exchange → Nurture → Book a call

Low-friction value exchange options in order of preference:
- WhatsApp opt-in ("Send us a message and we'll send you the free guide") — lowest friction, highest quality signal, keeps conversation in a channel people already use
- Free guide download with email capture — slightly higher friction, useful for email nurture
- Specific useful landing page (not a homepage, not a membership pitch — a page that answers the exact question the ad raised)

The Calendly link belongs at the end of the nurture sequence, offered to people who have already engaged with a piece of content or exchanged messages, not as the first CTA.

#### 4.13.3 Message Framework

The Dec–Feb campaign drove toward membership ("healthcare in Bali, sorted" register). That is a solution message shown to a cold audience that doesn't yet know they have a problem Padma Care solves.

The message hierarchy that works for this product:

Problem-aware cold audience (geofenced near hospital, or matching expat profile): Meet them at the anxiety. "Don't know what's happening with your family member in an Indonesian hospital? You don't have to figure this out alone." No membership mention. CTA is the free guide or WhatsApp.

Warm audience (retargeted people who visited the site or engaged with content): Introduce the service. "This is what Padma Care does and who it's for." CTA is a free 15-minute triage call — not a Calendly booking, a WhatsApp message to schedule.

Hot audience (retargeted people who engaged with warm content): Membership offer. Social proof. Specific outcome stories.

Each layer is a separate campaign with separate creative. This is where UGC matters most — a genuine 60-second testimonial from a Bali expat describing a specific stressful situation that Padma Care resolved outperforms any polished creative at the warm and hot layers.

#### 4.13.4 Agentic Optimization Loop

Once the corrected funnel is live and generating data, a campaign optimization agent integrated into the pmg-integrations bus handles the mechanical optimization. Not a new tool — Meta Marketing API calls integrated as a service alongside existing integrations.

**What the agent does autonomously:**
- Reads campaign performance daily via Meta Marketing API
- Produces terse performance digest: what's working, what's not, what dropped below threshold
- Pauses ad sets that fall below defined CTR or CPL thresholds after minimum impression volume
- Reallocates budget within approved ranges between performing ad sets
- Surfaces next test hypothesis with draft creative variants for approval

**What requires human approval:**
- New creative (copy, image, video) — agent drafts, human approves before deployment
- Budget increases above defined threshold
- New audience definitions
- Any change to the funnel structure or landing pages

**What the agent never does:**
- Spend above approved weekly budget cap
- Create new campaigns without explicit instruction
- Change targeting to include audiences outside the approved segment definitions

**Test cadence:** Minimum 500 impressions per variant before evaluation. Minimum 7-day run before pausing. One variable changed per test — audience OR creative OR CTA, never multiple simultaneously.

**First test hypothesis (post-audit):** Geofenced 0.5-mile radius around BIMC and Kasih Ibu, expat behavioral segment layered, problem-aware message, WhatsApp CTA. $100 budget. Measure cost per WhatsApp initiation. Compare to previous campaign baseline. This is a targeting and funnel test, not a creative test — hold creative constant from whatever performed best in the Dec–Feb audit.

**Integration path:** Meta Marketing API → pmg-integrations Node.js service → Bernard digest. Same pattern as existing integrations. Meta API access requires app approval (1–3 days). Marketing API permissions are separate from the standard Graph API and require business verification.

### 4.11 Events (Padma-Hosted)
**Segments:** B  
**Complexity:** High — logistics, venue, promotion, follow-up  
**Impact:** Medium — harder to scale than speaking at existing organizations

Hosting events is high overhead for uncertain return in Bali's pitch-fatigued environment. The more efficient version is speaking at events that already have the right audience (Section 4.3) rather than building an audience from scratch.

If events are pursued, the format that avoids the pitch-fatigue problem is utility-first: a free health screening morning, a "know your numbers" blood pressure and blood sugar check event, something where the value is entirely in what people receive, not in what they hear about Padma Care. Dr. Gita's presence is the brand statement.

Phase 3 at earliest. Not a priority while speaking circuit is still being developed.

## 6. Metrics That Matter

Not vanity metrics. These measure whether channels are converting.

- Inbound inquiries per channel (tracked at first contact — "how did you hear about us")
- Community responses posted per week
- Cost per inquiry for paid channels
- GP referral conversations (not formal referrals — just mentions)
- Speaking engagements per month
- Content pages indexed and appearing in AI search results
- Membership conversions traceable to a channel within 60 days

## 7. What This Is Not

- Not a brand awareness campaign — Padma Care is too early stage for awareness spend
- Not a social media follower growth strategy — followers don't pay memberships
- Not a villa/concierge network play — tested, wrong LTV profile
- Not a hospital partnership play (yet) — too slow, too resistant currently
- Not dependent on any single channel — resilience requires 3+ active channels simultaneously
